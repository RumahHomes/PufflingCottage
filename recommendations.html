Amble-by-the-Sea (& Coquet Estuary)

As recent arrivals & long-time visitors, we've compiled our favourite places to visit, eat, and explore. Here are our top recommendations to make the most of your stay in and around Amble-by-the-Sea.

⚓️

Amble (Coquet Estuary & Hauxley)

Amble Harbour

"The Friendliest Port" as named in the 1930s by RMS Mauretania on it's final journey, Amble sits at the mouth of the Coquet Estuary and remains a working port for trawlers & trips out to Couquet Island. Fresh catches can be purchased from Alliance Fish. The fish is seasonal so the counter changes with this in mind. Catches may consist of Langoustines, Cod, Haddock, Whiting, Monk, Halibut, Saithe, Lemon Sole, Plaice, Squid, Hake, Dover Sole, Mackerel, Herring, Red Gurnard, Red Mullet, Turbot or Brill.

📍 5-min walk ⏱️ Half-day exploring

Amble Beaches

Little Shore beach, with it's brightly coloured huts, is right in town, next to the lovely pier and harbour. With dogs you can walk south to Amble Links onward to Low Hauxley & even further toward Druridge Bay. All in the shadow of Coquet Island, home to puffins, Roseate terns, a seal colony and the mercurial dolphin pods that patrol this coastline. If you walk at low tide you can see the Low Hauxley Fossil Forest that dates from the Mesolithic period part of a, once, dry landmass (Doggerland) that connected Britain to Europe before being submerged by rising sea levels.

📍 Amble Links 15-min walk ⏱️ Half-day walking

Amble Bord Waalk

A trail of public sculptures inspired by the bird-life found around the Amble coast. The artworks have been created by nationally and internationally renowned artists, each bringing something unique and very special to the area. To experience the virtual, poetry & sound pieces you will need to download the app which also gives additional information on the artists and a location map.

📍 The 1st sculptures a 5-min walk away ⏱️ Takes as long as you need!

Amble Harbour Village

With 15 retail "pods" covering coffee & food outlets, gifts and local handicrafts, no visit to Amble is complete without a visit. You can also visit the Seafood centre & lobster hatchery. And, it's the base for Puffin Cruises.

📍 5-min walk away ⏱️ Shop as long as you want!

Coquet Island & Puffin Cruises

Coquet Island is a vital RSPB nature reserve off the Northumberland coast, famous for holding the UK's only breeding colony of Roseate Terns and home to 40,000 breeding Puffins in spring & summer every year. Puffin Cruises have a pod where you can book your trip (in advance to avoid disappointment) or even watch "puffin cam" whilst you wait!

📍 The harbour is a 5-min walk ⏱️ Each trip ~1 hour

Water Sports & The Coquet Estuary

If you'd like to try some wind-surfing, sailing, canoeing, SUP or kayaking then contact Coquet Canoe Club. Whilst you are paddling up river, see if you can make it to The Hermitage beyond Warkworth Castle or hang in the estuary where you might spot otters and even the seals have been known to make it over the weir toward Warkworth! It is also possible, in good conditions, to paddle out Coquet Island.

📍 River Coquet 10-min walk ⏱️ Take as long as you need
🦦

Nature & Wildlife

Hauxley Wildlife Discovery Centre

Part of the Northumberlad Wildlife Trust & one of the best wildlife watching spots in the North East (other NWT sites close by at Druridge Pools & East Chevington). At Hauxley visitors can enjoy the walks and nature trails around the reserve, the tranquillity of the hides & tasty something from The Lookout Café (try the scones!).

📍 Low Hauxley ⏱️ Free, spend as long as you like!

Druridge Bay Country Park

Druridge Bay is a living landscape, rich in wildlife and with big plans for the future. The bay is a stunning seven mile stretch of sand running from Amble to Cresswell. The park is centred on a lake with surrounding meadows and woods which has been restored from an old opencast coal mine.

📍 5-min drive ⏱️ Great for picnics & cycling

St Oswald's Way & the Northumberland Coast Path

Section 4 of St. Oswald's Way wanders up the scenic Coquet valley just inland from Amble on one of the longest sections of the route. It passes through Felton & woodland on the southern side of the Coquet to reach Rothbury, "the Capital of Coquetdale", set in superb Northumberland scenery. Amble is toward the end of Stage 1 of the Northumberland Coast Path. Stage 2 from Warkworth to Craster via Alnmouth is lovely too!

📍 10-min walk from cottage to intercept both ⏱️ Full, active day for full stages
🍽️

Food & Drink

Jasper's Bistro

Join Ryan & the dedicated team in an intimate setting with the freshest of seafood cooked to order from their open kitchen. Great "Specials" & booking well in advance is essential! (We love the whisky-pairing dishes & the mountain chalet style of the window table)

📍 5-min walk 🌟 Local favourite

Sonnet

Named in the Michelin Guide & with many award wins Sonnet is a must if you love great food. They serve either an 8-course lunch or 14-course tasting menu at dinner; focused on the best quality ingredients, with the emphasis being on 'simplicity'. (We prefer to visit at lunch).

📍 Alnwick, 20-min drive 🌟 Award-winning!

Fish Shack

Part of the Old Boat House group & situated on Amble's quayside, Fish Shack is a cosy, rustic eatery that was purpose built using the old 'Sea Quest' boat & boasts great views too! Their sister restaurant is near the pods.

📍 10-min walk 🌟 Casual dining

Brewis Beer Co Taproom

Local family run microbrewery & taproom (the only place to sell their wide variety of great beers. Open Thurs-Sun & with outside catering food vans this is a great local independent business.

📍 5-min walk from cottage 🌟 Local independent brewery

Harbour Fish Bar

Not the only "chippy" in Amble, but it is our preferred option for all the "classics" and a few typical regional offerings too. When busy, get there early before the fish runs out! It can be that popular; with queues round the block.

📍 5-min walk 🌟 Great local chippy

Other local businesses

So many local businesses need our support. We are selecting only a few here. Hummingbird is a cool "ready-to-drink" mixologist company well worth a vist when open (or buy their wares from local retailers, we love Grounded their cold brew coffee liquer). Ethos cafe, next to Hummingbird is another great new addition to Amble. There are any number of small independent cafe's & bars; we also enjoy a Friday crab sandwich from Gracy's and the wonderful cakes from The Old Customs House Tearooms. Venture a few miles inland to try out the Morwick Dairy for great farm-produced, hand-made ice cream.

📍 All in Amble 🌟 Support local businesses!

Practical Information

Nearest Supermarkets

Amble has a Tesco for essentials & a short walk from the cottage a larger Morrisons supermarket. For more options, Alnwick (20 min) has Turnbulls, M&S Foodhall and Aldi.

📍 Various

Vets & Doctors

The nearest vet is a few minutes walk or drive from the cottage. We can provide contact details and directions on request – just ask when you book! There is Coquet Medical Group for GP access and the nearest hospital is in Alnwick for minor injuries & the nearest A&E is at Cramlington; Northumbria Specialist Emergency Care Hospital

Beach Safety

Most beaches are dog-friendly year-round. Check tide times before heading to Holy Island. Lifeguards patrol some beaches in summer months.

Coast

Explore the stunning Northumberland coastline, designated an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty & more recently a National Landscape. From dramatic dunes to pristine sandy bays, discover some of Britain's most spectacular coastal scenery.

Cresswell

At the southern end of Druridge Bay sits Cresswell, a great stretch of beach, with a few car parking options & the ever popular Drift Cafe

Warkworth

About 1 mile out of the town and near the golf course is one of the often bypassed beaches & yet a beautiful beach with great dunes & some rockpools and so close to Puffling Cottage!

Alnmouth

A picturesque coastal village at the mouth of the River Aln, known for its colourful houses, sandy beach, and excellent estuary walks.

Craster

Famous for L. Robson & Sons kippers a 4th generation family business where they still cure the herrings in the original smokehouses which are over 130 years old. From Craster you can begin the stunning mile-long coastal walk to Dunstanburgh Castle

Embleton Bay

Embleton Bay can be approached from Craster via Dunstanburgh Castle or from Embleton village & through the golf course or from the north and Low Newton (visit The Telegraph's "best pub in Northumberland", The Ship Inn)

Beadnell

Great bay for watersports (kite surfing etc), with lime kilns at one end and vast dunes south toward Long Nanny Burn a great place to walk. In Beadnell itself wonderful food can found at Beadnell Towers. Beadnell Harbour is the only west-facing harbour in Northumberland!

Seahouses

Seahouses is a bustling fishing harbour & is known as the gateway to the Farne Islands

Farne Islands

National Trust operated National Nature Reserve & Site of Special Scientific Interest. Home to an internationally significant breeding colony of seabirds (esp Puffins!) and Atlantic grey seals.

Bamburgh Beach

One of the finest beaches in England, with Bamburgh Castle as a dramatic backdrop. Miles of golden sand, perfect for walking, playing and rock pooling.

Budle Bay

The 1 mile wide bay forms part of the Lindisfarne National Nature Reserve. Famous for waders, wildfowl, and migrating birds on its mudflats, particularly when the tide is out.

Other Beaches

There are many more beaches to be found & explored along the National Landscape of the great Northumberland coastline. Sugar Sands, Football Hole, Cocklawburn & Howick Sands are just a few of them

Golf Links

From The Open qualifying course of Goswick GC, to the "best-value" golf course in the UK at Dunstanburgh Castle GC to the 9-hole hidden gems of Warkworth & Alnmouth Village - there are great golf options for all abilities and budgets all along the Northumberland coast

Castles

Northumberland boasts more castles than any other English county. From magnificent restored fortresses to dramatic clifftop ruins, explore centuries of history and power.

Warkworth Castle

Puffling Cottage is just a short walk along the River Coquet from the iconic Warkworth Castle & Hermitage, the home from 14th century onwards of the powerful Percy family. Discover how the great medieval baronial fortress-palace worked in its 15th-century heyday.

Bamburgh Castle

Royal Fortress, Norman Stronghold, Coastal Home. "Bebbanburg" has stood guard above the spectacular Northumberland coastline for over 1,400 years & spans 9 acres on its rocky plateau.

Dunstanburgh Castle

Dramatic ruins reached only on foot from Embleton Bay or Craster. Begun in 1313 by Earl Thomas of Lancaster & built on a massive scale, proclaiming his rivalry with the Crown.

Alnwick Castle

Origins date back to the Norman period & historians believe construction began around 1096. In recent times used as a film set, for Transformers, Downton Abbey & as Hogwarts for 2 Harry Potter films

The Holy Island of Lindisfarne

In 635AD St Aidan, invited by King Oswald, came from Iona & founded his monastery on the island of Lindisfarne. The masterpiece now known as the Lindisfarne Gospels was created here in the early 8th century. Lindisfarne is a tidal island reachable via a paved causeway only when the North Sea tides permit.

Cheviots & The Interior

Venture inland to discover rolling hills, ancient forests, and the wild beauty of Northumberland National Park and the Dark Sky Reserve.

Hadrian's Wall

UNESCO World Heritage Roman frontier. Discover the remains of the forts & towns that once kept watch over the 73-mile long wall at various locations

Kielder Water & Forest

Home to England's largest forest and the biggest man-made lake in Northern Europe

Northumberland International Dark Sky Park

At 580sq miles, Europe's second largest Dark Sky Reserve. Open year round
